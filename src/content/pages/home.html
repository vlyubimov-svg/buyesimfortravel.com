What "unlimited data" means on a Europe eSIM

An unlimited Europe eSIM gives you a fixed amount of full-speed data each day and keeps you online at a reduced speed after that. The plan does not cut you off during its validity period, but the speed after the cap decides what still works.

Three numbers define any unlimited plan:

Number 1

Daily allowance

The amount of 4G or 5G data you get each day before the slowdown. In this comparison it ranges from 1 GB (TonMobile) to 5 GB (Saily, Airalo Eurolink).

Number 2

Speed after the cap

TonMobile drops to 512 kbps. Saily, Airalo and Nomad drop to 1 Mbps. Messaging, maps, email and web pages keep working at both speeds.

Number 3

Reset time

The allowance renews every 24 hours, so a slow evening does not carry over to the next day.

Holafly works differently. It does not state a daily cap for phone data and relies on a fair use policy that lets local networks slow heavy users temporarily. Its hotspot sharing is capped at about 1 GB per day on prepaid plans, according to the Adam and Linds review from August 2026.

What 1 GB per day covers in practice

A typical sightseeing day is map navigation, messaging with photos, ride-hailing and booking apps, email and social media browsing. That fits inside 1 GB for many travellers, and the 512 kbps tail keeps the basics alive afterwards.

Travellers who upload many videos or stream music in high quality all day should look at a plan with a higher daily allowance. Video calls and HD streaming do not run smoothly at 512 kbps or at 1 Mbps.

Good to know

The speed after the cap is as important as the cap itself. A plan with 5 GB a day and 1 Mbps afterwards and a plan with 1 GB a day and 512 kbps afterwards are built for different days, not for different trips.

Single-country vs multi-country unlimited eSIMs

If 80% or more of your nights are in one country, a single-country unlimited plan is the cheaper route. A week in Paris with a day trip to Brussels, for example, works well with a TonMobile France unlimited plan plus a 1 GB Europe plan ($3.90) installed as a second eSIM for the day in Belgium.

A regional plan makes sense for rail passes, road trips and cruises that change country every day or two. Before buying, check that every country on your route is on the provider's list. Switzerland, the UK, Norway, Turkey and the Balkans are often missing from EU-only plans.

Hotspot and tethering on unlimited Europe eSIMs

Hotspot rules differ more than prices. Holafly caps sharing at about 1 GB per day. Saily, Airalo and Nomad do not list a separate hotspot cap on their Europe plans in the sources we checked, but shared data still counts toward the daily full-speed allowance.

TonMobile does not publish a separate hotspot limit for its unlimited plans. Check the plan details in the app before relying on your phone as a laptop modem.

Before you tether

If you plan to work from a laptop on a train, a 1 GB daily allowance disappears quickly once the laptop shares the connection. In that case a 5 GB plan, or a large fixed plan, is the safer choice.

On Android, the hotspot toggle sits in the quick settings panel: swipe down and tap Hotspot, per Google's support page. We cover the setup and the data side in more detail in the guides section.

Non-EU countries: UK, Switzerland, Turkey and the Balkans

EU roaming rules do not apply in the UK, Switzerland, Turkey, Serbia or North Macedonia, so many home carriers charge extra for roaming there. The European Commission's roaming page states that the "Roam Like At Home" regime covers the 27 EU states plus Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway, and that it does not apply when you travel to the UK. A travel eSIM avoids this, but only if the plan lists those countries.

How to choose the best unlimited eSIM for your Europe trip

Pick the plan by route first, then by daily usage, then by price per day.

  1. List every country on your route

    One main country points to a single-country unlimited plan. Three or more countries point to a regional plan.

  2. Estimate daily data use

    Under 2 GB a day, a fixed plan is usually cheaper than unlimited.

  3. Check the allowance and the speed after it

    512 kbps to 1 Mbps is enough for maps and messaging, not for video calls.

  4. Read the hotspot rule

    Do this if you plan to share data with a laptop or a travel partner.

  5. Compare price per day, not the headline price

    Divide the plan price by its number of days.

  6. Check the activation window and refund terms

    TonMobile gives 180 days to activate and reviews refunds when the eSIM cannot be installed or has no network in the declared coverage area, according to its terms.

  7. Confirm your phone supports eSIM and is not carrier-locked

    Our compatibility checklist walks through both.

How to set up a TonMobile eSIM for Europe

Setup takes a few minutes and is best done at home over Wi-Fi, before the flight.

  1. Open TonMobile

  6. Track and top up

    Remaining days and data are in the My eSIM section. Top up there without reinstalling.

FAQ

What is the best eSIM for Europe with unlimited data?

TonMobile is the best value for trips focused on one country, with unlimited plans listed from $3.10 per day and $16.50 for 7 days in France or the UK. Saily and Airalo Eurolink are better for multi-country routes with heavy use, offering 5 GB of full-speed data per day.

Is any Europe eSIM truly unlimited?

No. Every unlimited Europe eSIM slows down after a daily allowance or under a fair use policy. TonMobile slows to 512 kbps after 1 GB per day. Saily, Airalo and Nomad slow to 1 Mbps after 2 to 5 GB per day.

How much does an unlimited eSIM for Europe cost?

For 7 days, prices in this comparison range from $16.50 (TonMobile, France or UK) to $28.99 (Saily, 35 countries). For 30 days, TonMobile single-country plans cost $69.99 to $84.50 depending on the country, and regional plans from Saily, Airalo and Holafly cost $71.00 to $73.90.

Does one eSIM work across all of Europe?

Yes, with a regional plan. TonMobile's Europe plan works in 35 countries with fixed data up to 100 GB. Airalo Eurolink covers 41 countries with unlimited data. Single-country unlimited plans work only in the country you bought them for.

Do I need a passport or ID to buy a Europe eSIM?

Not with TonMobile, which states that it requires no documents at purchase. Rules at other providers and in some countries can differ, so check before buying.

Can I pay for a Europe eSIM with crypto?

Yes. TonMobile accepts cryptocurrency, including Bitcoin and Toncoin, alongside bank cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay.

Will I keep my phone number with a travel eSIM?

Yes. The unlimited Europe eSIMs in this list are data-only, so your home SIM stays active for calls and SMS, including bank codes. Roaming charges for calls on the home SIM still apply.

Does an unlimited eSIM work in the UK and Switzerland?

Only if the plan lists them. TonMobile includes both in its 35-country Europe plan and sells a UK unlimited plan from $3.10 per day.
