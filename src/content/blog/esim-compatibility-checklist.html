Is my phone eSIM compatible? Most recent phones are, but two checks matter before you buy: the phone must support eSIM, and it must not be locked to your home carrier. Do both at home, install the plan over Wi-Fi before you fly, and the border crossing becomes a non-event.

Key takeaways

  • Apple lists Dual SIM, including eSIM, for iPhone XS, XS Max, XR and later.
  • On Android, the phone maker's support page is the place to confirm eSIM support for your exact model.
  • A carrier-locked phone can refuse a second carrier. Ask your carrier to unlock it before you travel.
  • Install the eSIM on home Wi-Fi, then switch on the line and data roaming when you land.

How to know your phone supports eSIM

An eSIM is a SIM built into the phone and set up by software, so there is no card to swap at the airport. That only works if the hardware has an embedded SIM chip, and not every phone does.

The question is a yes or no per model. Check the official source for your device.

iPhone

According to Apple, Dual SIM, including eSIM, needs an iPhone XS, XS Max, XR or later. Apple's own page explains the details and is worth a quick read before you buy anything: Apple support, Dual SIM.

Android

Android makers each handle eSIM in their own way, and menu names differ between brands and software versions. So the reliable route is the support page of your phone's manufacturer: search for your exact model name and check that it lists eSIM. If the page is unclear, ask the maker's support before you pay for a plan.

Why the exact model matters

"Supports eSIM" is a statement about a specific model, not a brand. Two phones from the same maker can differ, so look up the full model name, not just the series.

Carrier lock explained

A carrier-locked phone is tied to one network. Phones sold on an instalment plan are a common example, though your carrier's terms decide it, not the phone's age. A lock can stop the phone from using another carrier's plan, including a travel eSIM.

For Apple, the rule is stated plainly: to use two carriers, the carrier must unlock the iPhone, or both plans must come from the same carrier. A travel eSIM from a different provider falls into the first case. If your iPhone is locked, the unlock has to come from your carrier, not from the eSIM seller.

Unlocked phone

  • Accepts a plan from a second provider
  • Travel eSIM installs as normal
  • Nothing to ask your carrier

Carrier-locked phone

  • May refuse a different provider's plan
  • Carrier has to unlock it first
  • Start the request well before departure

Do not leave the unlock to the last week

Carriers set their own conditions and timing for unlocking. We cannot tell you how long yours takes, so ask early and get confirmation in writing if you can. Then check again that the phone accepts a second line.

Install over Wi-Fi at home

Installing an eSIM needs an internet connection, and the safest place for that is your home Wi-Fi a few days before departure. An airport or a train platform in a new country is a bad place to discover that something is wrong, because you have no data to fix it with.

Installing early also gives you time to sort out the problems described below. If the phone says it cannot add the plan, you can still contact the seller, or your carrier about the lock, while you have a working home line.

Once the plan is on the phone, leave the new line switched off for data until you land, so nothing starts counting before the trip does. To stay reachable on your usual number, keep the home SIM for calls; the guide to keeping your home number with a data-only eSIM explains how that split works.

Step by step: installing your eSIM

The exact screens depend on your phone and provider, so treat this as the generic order of events. Your provider's own instructions always win where they differ.

  1. Buy the plan and get the install details

    After checkout, the provider gives you either an in-app install button or an eSIM QR code. Keep that screen or email open. If you use TonMobile, the app includes an in-app installation guide, so you can follow it on the same device.

  2. Connect to Wi-Fi

    Stay on home Wi-Fi for the whole install. Do not rely on your mobile data for this step.

  3. Install through the app or scan the QR code

    Follow the app's prompt, or scan the QR code from the phone's own add-eSIM option. If your phone is the same device that displays the code, save the code as an image, or open it on a second screen such as a laptop or tablet.

  4. Label the new line

    Give the plan a name you will recognise, so you can tell it apart from your home line in settings.

  5. Turn on the travel line for data

    Choose the travel eSIM as the line used for mobile data. Your home SIM can stay as the line for calls and messages.

  6. Enable data roaming for the travel line

    Switch on data roaming for the eSIM line only. This is the setting most often missed, and it is the first thing to check if nothing loads abroad.

Store the confirmation email and the install screen where you can reach them offline. If you ever need to reinstall, you will want that code, and you may not have signal when you do.

What to do when you land

When the plane lands, you only need to switch the travel line on and check that data roaming is enabled for it. Your phone should then find a local network on its own.

That daily allowance is worth understanding before you rely on the phone for maps and video. Reduced speeds after the allowance keep maps, messaging, email and web pages working, while video calls and HD streaming do not run smoothly. No network after landing

Start with the simplest cause: data roaming must be on for the travel line. It sounds backward, because you are not roaming on the plan itself, but the phone still treats a foreign network as roaming. If it is on and nothing loads, restart the phone and confirm the travel line is the one selected for data.

The eSIM cannot be installed at all

If the phone refuses the plan after you have checked model support and lock status, contact the seller. Pre-flight checklist

Run through this list a week before departure, and again the night before you fly.

  • Phone confirmed as not carrier-locked, or unlock requested from your carrier
  • eSIM plan bought, with coverage for every country on your route

FAQ

Is my phone eSIM compatible if it is an iPhone?

According to Apple, Dual SIM with eSIM needs an iPhone XS, XS Max, XR or later. Check Apple's support page for the current list before you buy.

How do I check eSIM support on an Android phone?

Look up your exact model on the phone maker's support page and confirm that it lists eSIM. Menu names differ between brands, so the maker's page is more reliable than a general guide.

Can a carrier-locked phone use a travel eSIM?

It may not. For iPhone, Apple says the carrier must unlock the phone to use two carriers, unless both plans come from the same carrier. Ask your carrier for the unlock before you travel.

Can I install an eSIM before my flight?

Yes, and it is the safest way to do it. Install over home Wi-Fi a few days ahead, keep the travel line off for data, and turn it on when you land.

What is an eSIM QR code and can I scan it from the same phone?

It is the install code your provider gives you after purchase. If it is displayed on the phone you want to install on, open it on a second screen or use the provider's in-app install instead.

What if the eSIM will not install or has no network?

Check model support, lock status and data roaming on the travel line first. Then contact the provider: TonMobile's Terms say refunds are reviewed when an eSIM cannot be installed or has no network in the declared coverage area.
