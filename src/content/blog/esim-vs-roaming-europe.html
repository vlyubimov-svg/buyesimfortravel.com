For a short trip inside the EU, your home plan may already work abroad at no extra charge, so roaming can be enough. A travel eSIM wins when your carrier charges for roaming, when you cross into the UK or other non-covered countries, or when you want a separate data allowance. A local SIM is the slowest route and rarely worth it for a multi-country trip.

Key takeaways

  • Roam Like At Home covers the 27 EU states plus Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway, with Ukraine and Moldova added from 1 January 2026. It does not apply in the UK.
  • Operators may apply fair-use limits on data, so "included" does not always mean unlimited.
  • A travel eSIM is data-only and sits beside your home SIM, so your number stays active.
  • A local SIM means a new number, a shop visit and a separate purchase in every country.
  • Check your own contract first. The rules set a floor, but your carrier decides the details.

What each option is

The three choices solve the same problem in different ways: getting mobile data abroad without a nasty bill. The difference is who supplies the connection and what happens to your number.

Home-carrier roaming

  • You keep your normal SIM and number.
  • Your carrier pays a foreign network and passes the cost on, or absorbs it.
  • Nothing to buy, but terms depend on your contract.

Travel eSIM

  • A digital SIM you install by QR code or app.
  • Usually data-only, with no phone number of its own.
  • Bought before you leave, priced per country, region or data amount.

The third option is a local SIM: a physical card from a carrier in the country you visit. It gives you a local number and local rates, but you have to find a shop, hand over the card swap and repeat the process in each country.

EU roaming rules and what they cover

The rule behind most of this is "Roam Like At Home". According to the European Commission's roaming page, it applies in the 27 EU member states plus Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway. Ukraine and Moldova are covered from 1 January 2026.

In practice, a traveller whose plan is from one of those countries can use it in the others at domestic terms. That is the heart of "roam like at home": the trip should not cost more per minute or per gigabyte than staying put.

The UK is not included

Roam Like At Home does not apply in the United Kingdom. Anyone searching for EU roaming rules UK travellers can rely on should read their own carrier's terms, because the EU rule does not decide them. The Commission page also does not mention Switzerland, so check that one with your carrier too.

Fair use still applies

The Commission notes that operators may apply fair-use limits on data. That means an "unlimited" home plan can still be restricted abroad once you pass a threshold your carrier sets. The threshold is in your contract or on your carrier's roaming page, not in the EU rule itself.

Source: European Commission, roaming policy page, read on 27 October 2026.

When roaming is enough

Roaming is the right answer more often than travel-eSIM marketing admits. It costs nothing extra to set up and nothing can go wrong with a QR code.

  • Your plan was issued in an EU, Iceland, Liechtenstein or Norway market, and you are staying in that zone.
  • Your carrier's roaming page confirms the destination at domestic terms.
  • Your daily use is light: maps, messages, a few web pages.
  • You are away for a few days, not weeks.
  • You do not plan to tether a laptop for long stretches.

Before you fly, open your carrier's app or website and find the roaming section. Look for the fair-use allowance, any hotspot restriction and the list of covered countries. Ten minutes there is worth more than any comparison table, including this one.

When a travel eSIM wins

A travel eSIM tends to win in a handful of recognisable situations. None of them need a technical reason; they come from where the roaming rule stops.

Crossing into the UK

Rule does not follow you

If the trip includes London or Edinburgh, home roaming may be priced differently than in the EU. A UK or Europe plan fixes that in advance.

Heavy data

Fair use gets in the way

If you tether or stream a lot, a plan with its own allowance can be easier to plan around than a fair-use cap you cannot see.

Home plan from outside the zone

Roaming is the expensive path

Visitors from outside Europe do not get Roam Like At Home at all, so a regional eSIM is often the first thing to check.

Multi-country routes

One install, many borders

Local SIM drawbacks

A local SIM looks cheap on paper, and in a single-country stay it can be a reasonable choice. For most trips, though, the friction adds up.

Pros

  • Local number for local deliveries and bookings.
  • Local network terms, with a human at the counter if something fails.
  • Works with phones that do not support eSIM.

Cons

  • You must find a shop, often on arrival when you are tired and offline.
  • Some countries ask for ID when you register a SIM, so check the local rules.
  • Your home SIM comes out, or you need a dual-SIM phone.
  • Every new country means another SIM, another top-up and another plan to read.
  • The new number is useless when you leave.

On a rail trip through four countries, that is four shop visits. A regional eSIM installed at home before the flight avoids all of them.

Keeping your home number

Most people want one thing above all: to stay reachable on their usual number. Roaming does that automatically. A local SIM does not, unless you carry a second phone or use a dual-SIM setup.

A data-only travel eSIM sits in the middle. It gives you internet while your home SIM stays installed for calls and texts. TonMobile's plans, for example, are data-only with no phone number, according to its listing, so they are built for exactly this arrangement. We cover the setup in keeping your home number with a data-only eSIM.

Check dual-SIM support first

Apple says Dual SIM, including eSIM, needs an iPhone XS, XS Max, XR or later. To use two different carriers, the carrier must unlock the iPhone, unless both plans come from the same carrier. The eSIM compatibility checklist walks through the check on both iPhone and Android.

One practical habit: when a travel eSIM is your data line, turn off data roaming on the home SIM in your phone settings. That stops the phone from quietly using the more expensive connection when the eSIM is not selected.

FAQ

Is eSIM better than roaming in Europe?

Not always. Inside the zone covered by Roam Like At Home, roaming can cost nothing extra, so it may be enough. An eSIM is better when you visit the UK, when your carrier's terms are unclear or restrictive, or when you want a data allowance you can plan around.

Does Roam Like At Home apply in the UK?

No. According to the European Commission, the rule applies in the 27 EU states plus Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway, with Ukraine and Moldova covered from 1 January 2026. The UK is not on that list, so check your carrier's own terms for UK trips.

What does fair use mean for roaming data?

Operators may apply fair-use limits on data used abroad. The exact threshold sits in your contract or on your carrier's roaming page. Read it before you travel if you rely on mobile data heavily.

Is a travel eSIM cheaper than a local SIM?

It depends on the country and the plan, so compare current prices at checkout. The clearer difference is effort: an eSIM is installed before departure, while a local SIM means a shop visit, possible ID registration and a new number in each country.

Can I keep my home number with a travel eSIM?

Yes, if your phone supports dual SIM. A data-only eSIM provides internet while the home SIM stays active for calls and texts. Apple's Dual SIM feature needs an iPhone XS, XS Max, XR or later.

Do I need documents to buy a travel eSIM?

That depends on the provider. According to TonMobile's listing, no documents or identity check are needed at purchase. Check the terms of whichever provider you choose before paying.
